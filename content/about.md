I am a Signal Analysis Engineer at Jet Propulsion Laboratory ([JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov)) witin the [Radar Science and Engineering](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/radar-science/). As a Signal Analysis engineer, I work as a geospatial data scientist, applying tools coming from varied disciplines including deep learning, network science, statistics, and computer vision to design algorithms and analyze signals for Syntehtic Aperture Radar (SAR) remote sensing at a global scale. I currently work on the JPL projects Observational Products for End-Users from Remote Sensing Analysis ([OPERA](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/opera)) and Advanced Rapid Imaging and Analysis ([ARIA](https://aria.jpl.nasa.gov/)). It is a privilege to be apart of these projects.

On OPERA, I currently support the Disturbance from Sentinel-1 ([DIST-S1](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/opera/products/dist-product-suite/)) product, which is part of the SAR and optical disturbance suite. We delineate surface disturbances comparing recent acquisitions to a baseline of historical images. The input data is the [OPERA Radiometrically and Terrain corrected imagery](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/opera/products/rtc-product/). The disturbance product is a powerful tool to help delineate hazards such as fires and floods after a Sentinel-1 pass. The open-source software for DIST-S1 surface disturbance can be found [here](https://github.com/opera-adt/dist-s1). I have also supported validation activities, documenting requirements for the project and writing software to verify the requirements were met. I worked on the latter for the Dynamic Surface Water Extent ([DSWx](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/go/opera/products/dswx-product-suite/)) products. Validation entails ensuring that the OPERA products meet minimum accuracy and quality requirements using independent datasets and measurements. The open-source software for the DSWx product suite can be found [here](https://github.com/OPERA-Cal-Val/DSWx-Requirement-Verification). 

On [ARIA](https://aria.jpl.nasa.gov/), I support the cloud processing that generates [Sentinel-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel-1) interferograms and related products for tectonically active areas. We have developed many open-source [tools](https://github.com/ACCESS-Cloud-Based-InSAR) to better enable such processing in the cloud. We use [ISCE2](https://github.com/isce-framework/isce2-docs/blob/master/Notebooks/UNAVCO_2020/TOPS/topsApp.ipynb) to process Sentinel-1 [TOPS imagery](https://asf.alaska.edu/data-sets/sar-data-sets/sentinel-1/sentinel-1-what-is-topsar/) and [RAiDER](https://github.com/dbekaert/RAiDER) to perform tropospheric corrections. These [interferometric products](https://search.asf.alaska.edu/#/?dataset=SENTINEL-1%20INTERFEROGRAM%20(BETA)&resultsLoaded=true&granule=S1-GUNW-A-R-160-tops-20201020_20191020-154904-32061N_30175N-PP-7db9-v2_0_3-amplitude) can be searched and downloaded from the Alaska Satellite Facility data page. It is thanks to the efforts of [David Bekaert](https://www.davidbekaert.com/), who served as the ARIA project scientist until 2025, that we grew the archive from just over 200,000 products to nearly [1.1 million](https://search.asf.alaska.edu/#/?dataset=SENTINEL-1%20INTERFEROGRAM%20(BETA)&productTypes=GUNW_STD&resultsLoaded=true&granule=S1-GUNW-A-R-018-tops-20230528_20230516-232801-00072W_00037S-PP-2468-v2_0_6).

Before becoming a signal analysis engineer, I was a JPL postdoctoral fellow working under the supervision of [Marc Simard](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=JQJF1wgAAAAJ&hl=en) using airborne and satellite [SAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic-aperture_radar) imagery to estimate biomass in dense tropical forests, map mangrove extents, and detect forest disturbances for L-band imagery (in preparation for [NISAR](https://nisar.jpl.nasa.gov/)) using data from [ALOS-1](https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS/en/about/palsar.htm), [ALOS-2](https://www.eorc.jaxa.jp/ALOS-2/en/about/palsar2.htm), and [UAVSAR](https://uavsar.jpl.nasa.gov/). In addition, we created remote sensing [tutorials](https://github.com/simard-landscape-lab/sari-tutorial) for NASA's [Land-Cover and Land-Use Change program](https://lcluc.umd.edu/) to foster international remote sensing collaborations.

I earned my Ph.D. at UCLA under the supervision of [Andrea Bertozzi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~bertozzi/) and [Mason Porter](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~mason/) in applied and computational mathematics. I also worked closely with [Puck Rombach](http://www.uvm.edu/~mrombach/) during that time. I modeled processes in education, ecology, and organized crime using tools from [network science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_science). Additionally, I interned with [Igor Yanovsky](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~yanovsky/) in the summer of 2016 applying [Le-Vese segmentation](http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.75.8251&rep=rep1&type=pdf) to [MISR](https://misr.jpl.nasa.gov/) images. Before coming to JPL as a postdoc, I worked at the advertising startup Operam as a data scientist measuring online engagement for clients in film and television.
